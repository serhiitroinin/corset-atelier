Editor’s letter

On being held together, literally

Fashion has spent a decade telling you to relax. Elasticated waists, forgiving knits, trousers with the ambition of pyjamas. We have read the memo and we decline.

A corset is the only garment that arrives with a point of view. It edits. It cuts the rambling paragraph of an outfit down to one sharp sentence, and it does so with steel, coutil and rather a lot of hand stitching.

This issue is our entire argument: six pieces, four silhouettes, and one questionnaire that will tell you your size with more honesty than your jeans ever have. Wear it over a shirt to the office. Wear it under nothing to dinner. Either way, stand up straight. You will have no choice.

— V., Editor-in-Chief

Anatomy of a corset

“If it bends when you sit, it is a costume. Ours bend when we say so.”— Head of the workroom, unprompted

A proper corset is closer to bridge-building than dressmaking. Here is where the money goes, in five parts.

  1. 1

    The bones

    Twenty to twenty-eight of them. Spiral steel where you curve, flat steel where you must not. Tipped, capped and sealed in their own channels so nothing ever works loose.

  2. 2

    The busk

    A two-part sprung steel front closure with five loops and five pins. It is how you get in and out unaided, with dignity intact.

  3. 3

    The waist tape

    A hidden band of twill at the narrowest point that takes the full strain of the lacing. It is why size 24 is still size 24 in five years.

  4. 4

    The layers

    Fashion fabric, a herringbone coutil strength layer, and a cotton lining against the skin. Three layers, one of which you will ever see.

  5. 5

    The hand finishing

    Binding stitched by hand, eyelets set in pairs with a press, flossing at every bone end. About eleven hours per corset. We timed it.
