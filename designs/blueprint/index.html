§ 01 — Assembly

Seven layers. None of them decorative.

Exploded view · drag to separate
0 mm
  1. Outer fabricWool barathea, silk duchesse or cotton sateen. Carries no load; that is why it stays smooth.
  2. Coutil strength layerHerringbone cotton, 310 g/m². Zero stretch on grain. The actual corset.
  3. Waist tape25 mm petersham, stitched through every seam. Stops the waist growing by even a millimetre.
  4. Spiral steel bones7 mm, flex in two axes. Placed on curves: side, hip, bust.
  5. Flat steel bonesSprung steel, 12 mm. Either side of the grommets and busk, where nothing should bend.
  6. BuskStainless, 13 mm wide, five loop-and-pin. Rated well past anything lacing can apply.
  7. LiningCotton lawn, floated. Replaceable after a decade of wear.

§ 05 — Construction

What holds, and why it keeps holding.

Journal · Note 014

Every Vesna is cut on grain, seamed twice and finished by one maker, who signs the inside of the waist tape. The numbers below are the ones we check before it leaves.

Bones7 mm spiral on curves, 12 mm flat sprung steel at busk and lacing. Tips dipped, never capped. 24–32 per corset
BuskStainless, 13 mm, five loop-and-pin, underlaid with a 25 mm modesty placket
Waist tapePetersham, caught in every seam. ±3 mm at waist after 100 h wear
LayersFashion fabric fused to herringbone coutil, floating cotton lawn lining. Two layers of coutil on sizes 30+
GrommetsTwo-part brass, 5 mm, set at 22 mm pitch; 16 mm at the waist for pull loops
Hand finishingBinding felled by hand, bone channels bar-tacked, flossing on request. ≈ 6 h of 19
